# STFR
## Operator's Manual
### First Edition

So you have decided to operate an STFR. This book takes you from inspection to a safe shutdown.

---

## Part I. Construction

The STFR has six sections around its core. The casing, turbines, fuel slots, ports, and control network must work together.

Inspect every system before the first start. Being able to turn the reactor on does not mean it is ready.

---

## 1. Six Sections

Build all six sections. An incomplete reactor may start, but its behavior will be unpredictable and a normal shutdown may fail.

---

## 2. Structure Analyzer

The structure analyzer sits below the reactor. Use an empty hand to switch its projection on or off.

Compare every block with the projection. Clear marked spaces and replace incorrect parts.

---

## 3. Analysis Wand

The analysis wand checks construction and the orientation of marked blocks.

On a suitable hatch, right click to restore its reference position. Sneak and right click to change whether it is open.

---

## Part II. Controls

Connect the reactor port, mainframes, transmitters, receivers, and breakers with data cable.

The network carries commands to the reactor and readings to monitors. It needs power.

---

## 4. Mainframes

Each mainframe adds logic slots. Ports, transmitters, receivers, and alarms use those slots.

Mainframes can form a vertical column. One data cable connection to the column is enough.

---

## 5. Check the Network

Use the network load analyzer on a mainframe. It shows installed and occupied slots, power, and devices without a place on the network.

Fix network faults before investigating the core.

---

## 6. KEY and SSTB

Feed redstone to configured sides of a transmitter. Assign the KEY and SSTB channels with the signal modifier.

KEY permits a start. SSTB must receive a separate pulse for each action.

---

## 7. Monitor

Attach a monitor to a logic receiver. Right click it to open the editor.

With the preset wand, sneak and right click to choose a preset, then right click a monitor to apply it. For “NO SIGNAL,” check the receiver, cable, and power.

---

## Part III. Supplies

Use deuterium for the first start. Place fuel capsules in the slots and lock them.

An empty or unlocked capsule leaves its section in refueling mode.

---

## 8. Ports

Ports bring in water, lubricant, and energy. Steam and reaction products leave through them.

Right click a port with an empty hand to see its contents. Keep water flowing and leave an outlet for steam.

---

## 9. Startup Power

During confirmed startup, the reactor needs 1,000,000 units of energy each operating cycle.

A long shortage aborts the start or causes a failure. Check the supply before confirmation.

---

## Part IV. The Shift

Before starting, check the six sections, capsules, water, lubricant, power, and network again.

Watch the monitors and listen to announcements during the sequence.

---

## 10. Start

Turn on KEY and pulse SSTB once. Wait for the hatches to lock and all 15 breakers to close.

Give SSTB a second, separate pulse. Wait for ONLINE.

---

## 11. When Start Fails

Check network load, fuel capsules, power, and hatch state.

Do not pulse SSTB blindly. Read the mainframe message and remove the cause of the failure.

---

## 12. Operation

Watch fuel feed, core temperature, shield, and total stress. Change controls a little at a time and wait for the instruments to respond.

If the shield falls or stress rises, restore those readings first.

---

## 13. Shutdown

For a normal shutdown, the shield must be at least 60% and total stress no more than 20%.

Turn off KEY and pulse SSTB. Wait for a full stop and unlocked hatches before maintenance.

---

## 14. Alarms

The reactor has several emergency states. Each calls for a different response.

Read announcements, check the network and reactor instruments, then follow the stated procedure. Random button presses only get in the way.
